## 1. Executive Summary

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) represent a diverse spectrum of viral, bacterial, protozoal, fungal, ectoparasitic, and enteric pathogens transmitted through genital, oral, anal, manual, and skin-to-skin contact. The landscape of STI prevention and epidemiology has undergone transformative shifts due to biomedical advancements including:
* **U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)** for HIV via Antiretroviral Therapy (ART).
* **Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)**: Oral daily (TDF/FTC, TAF/FTC), 2-1-1 event-driven oral PrEP, bi-monthly injectable Cabotegravir (Apretude), and twice-yearly injectable Lenacapavir (PURPOSE 1 & 2 trial breakthroughs).
* **Doxycycline Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (Doxy-PEP)**: Implementation of 200 mg oral doxycycline within 72 hours of condomless sex, pioneered citywide by the San Francisco Department of Public Health (SFDPH) in October 2022 and formally adopted by the CDC in 2024, resulting in dramatic reductions in bacterial STIs (chlamydia down ~25-50%, early syphilis down ~36-50%).
* **Cross-Protective Vaccines**: Meningococcal B (4CMenB) vaccine demonstrating 30–46% cross-protection against *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.
* **Comprehensive Condom Failure Modeling**: Quantitative risk stratification distinguishing **Perfect Use** (98–99% reduction for fluid-borne STIs) from **Typical Use** (85–90% reduction), incorporating real-world failure mechanisms such as breakage (1.5–4.0%), slippage (1.0–3.5%), late application/early removal (15–30%), and oil-induced latex dissolution.

## 3. Prophylactics & Prevention Modalities

### A. Mechanical Barrier Methods

1. **External Condoms (Latex, Polyisoprene, Polyurethane)**
   * **Mechanism**: Covers the penile shaft, capturing ejaculatory/pre-ejaculatory fluid and isolating mucosal tissues.
   * **Fluid-Borne Pathogens**: Perfect use protection **98%–99%**; Typical use protection **85%–90%**.
   * **Skin-to-Skin Pathogens**: Perfect use protection **65%–75%**; Typical use protection **40%–50%**.

2. **Internal Condoms (FC2 Female Condom)**
   * **Efficacy**: Perfect use protection **~95%**, typical use **~79%–85%**. Offers wider vulvar coverage.

3. **Dental Dams & Polyurethane Sheets**
   * **Efficacy**: Eliminates direct contact with vaginal secretions, anal mucus, and fecal matter during oral sex.

4. **Medical-Grade Gloves (Latex, Nitrile, Vinyl)**
   * **Efficacy**: Prevents mucosal micro-trauma during fisting/manual play; eliminates enteric and HCV transfer.

### B. Condom Failure Dynamics, Breakage, Slippage & Typical Use Modeling

Condom efficacy in clinical trials ("Perfect Use") differs substantially from real-world application ("Typical Use"). Understanding condom failure vectors is essential for calculating true population-level and individual risk.

```
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Physical Breakage        │ 1.5% – 4.0% per act  │ Insufficient lube, friction, old     │
│                          │                      │ condom, oil-based lube degradation   │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Complete Slippage        │ 1.0% – 3.5% per act  │ Incorrect size, loss of erection,    │
│ (Off inside partner)     │                      │ improper withdrawal without ring hold│
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤

│ (Delayed/Early Removal)  │                      │ early removal prior to ejaculation   │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤

```

#### Mathematical Failure Model for Adjusted Per-Act Risk

The adjusted risk per act under typical condom use ($R_{\text{typical}}$) combines the perfect-use protected risk ($R_{\text{protected}}$) and unprotected baseline risk ($R_{\text{unprotected}}$) weighted by total condom failure rate ($F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{break}} + F_{\text{slip}} + F_{\text{misuse}}$):

$$R_{\text{typical}} = (1 - F_{\text{total}}) \cdot R_{\text{protected}} + F_{\text{total}} \cdot R_{\text{unprotected}}$$

Where:
* $F_{\text{total}} \approx 0.043$ (4.3% aggregate physical failure per act in typical anal/vaginal intercourse).
* For HIV Receptive Anal Intercourse ($R_{\text{unprotected}} = 1.38\%$):
  * **Perfect Use Risk**: $R_{\text{protected}} = 1.38\% \times (1 - 0.98) = 0.028\%$ (1 in 3,571 acts).
  * **Typical Use Risk**: $R_{\text{typical}} = (1 - 0.043) \times 0.028\% + 0.043 \times 1.38\% = 0.086\%$ (1 in 1,162 acts).

#### Emergency Post-Failure Mitigation Protocols

When a condom breaks, slips off inside a partner, or is used incorrectly during a high-risk exposure:
1. **HIV PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)**: Initiate within 72 hours (ideally $<24\text{h}$). 28-day regimen of TDF/FTC + Dolutegravir reduces HIV acquisition risk by **>81%–89%**.
2. **Doxy-PEP (Bacterial STI PEP)**: Take a single 200 mg dose of doxycycline within 72 hours of condom breach. Reduces Chlamydia risk by **~88%**, Syphilis by **~87%**, and Gonorrhea by **~55%**.
3. **Emergency Contraception (EC)**: Levonorgestrel (Plan B, within 72h) or Ulipristal acetate (Ella, within 120h) for receptive vaginal condom failures.
4. **Post-Exposure Hepatitis B & Mpox Vaccination**: Administer HBIG + Hep B vaccine within 24h (unvaccinated), or JYNNEOS vaccine within 4–14 days for Mpox exposures.

### D. Lubricants and Mucosal Integrity Dynamics

* **Osmolality & Epithelial Damage**: High-osmolality lubricants ($>1,200 \text{ mOsm/kg}$) cause mucosal shedding and micro-ulcerations, raising HIV/HCV vulnerability. WHO recommends osmolarities $<1,200 \text{ mOsm/kg}$ (ideally $<350 \text{ mOsm/kg}$).
* **Nonoxynol-9 (N-9) Warning**: N-9 disrupts cell membranes and increases HIV transmission risk; strongly contraindicated.

## 5. Vaccinations for Sexually Transmissible Pathogens

1. **HPV Vaccine (Gardasil 9)**: **>98%–100%** prevention of target strains (6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58).
2. **Hepatitis B Vaccine (Heplisav-B)**: 2-dose series; **95%–100%** seroprotection.
3. **Hepatitis A Vaccine (Havrix)**: 2-dose series; **>95%–100%** seroprotection.
4. **Mpox Vaccine (JYNNEOS)**: 2-dose MVA-BN vaccine; **82%–89%** real-world effectiveness.
5. **Meningococcal B Vaccine Cross-Protection (4CMenB / Bexsero)**: **30%–46%** cross-protection against *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.

## 6. Demographic & Community Epidemiology

### San Francisco City & County Statistics
* **SFDPH Post Doxy-PEP Trends**: Chlamydia **▼ 25%**, Early Syphilis **▼ 36%**, Gonorrhea **▼ 3%**.
* **HIV Viral Suppression**: **>93%** of diagnosed residents virally suppressed (U=U).

### Gay, Bisexual, and MSM Demographics
* **Population Share**: ~12%–16% LGBTQ+ in SF. MSM represent >80% of early syphilis cases and >70% of extragenital (rectal/pharyngeal) STIs.

### Kink, BDSM, and Leather Communities
* **Non-Coital Vectors**: Manual play (fisting) without gloves/lube elevates HCV risk; nitrile glove use and equipment disinfection serve as essential harm reduction.

### Polyamorous, ENM, and Monogamous Relationship Structures
* **Prevalence**: 15%–25% in urban SF queer subcultures. High testing velocity (every 3 months) creates "epidemiological buffering" across fluid networks.

### Global Epidemiological Baselines
* **WHO Global Incidences**: Trichomoniasis (~156M/yr), Chlamydia (~129M/yr), Gonorrhea (~82M/yr), Syphilis (~7.1M/yr). HSV-1 (~3.7B under 50), HSV-2 (~491M aged 15-49).
